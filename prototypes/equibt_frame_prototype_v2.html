Why start here

Most improvement programmes solve the wrong problem.

The pattern is predictable. A symptom surfaces - customer complaints increase, cycle times blow out, costs exceed budget. Someone forms a hypothesis about what is wrong. A project gets funded based on that hypothesis. The project runs for six months. At the end, the original symptom is still there because the hypothesis was wrong.

The root cause is not laziness or poor methodology. It is misalignment. Different stakeholders genuinely see different problems because they sit in different parts of the operation. Without a structured process to reconcile these views, the loudest voice wins - or worse, a compromise emerges that addresses nobody's real concern.

FRAME prevents this. It applies SPIN methodology - Situation analysis, Problem identification, Implication mapping, and Non-Negotiable Needs and Payoffs - to produce a project charter with clear scope boundaries, success criteria, and stakeholder commitment.

Recent engagements.

Government and Public Sector FRAME

1 wk

Three-directorate alignment achieved in five days. Project charter signed before diagnostic began.

A government agency had three directorates with competing hypotheses about the root cause of a cross-functional service failure. Each directorate had commissioned its own preliminary analysis. No shared problem definition existed.

Ran a two-day SPIN workshop across all three directorates. Surfaced the three competing hypotheses and worked through each against available evidence. Produced a single agreed problem statement and a signed project charter within the week. The diagnostic that followed had clear targets and full stakeholder commitment.
Financial Services FRAME

3 views

Three competing views on the root cause aligned to one agreed problem statement before the diagnostic started.

A financial services operations team was about to commission a process redesign. Executive leadership had three different views on what was broken. The project had been approved on the assumption that the problem was understood.

Used FRAME before the diagnostic was commissioned. The SPIN workshop revealed that all three views were describing symptoms of the same root cause - a missing decision right at the point of exception handling. The project charter was reframed accordingly. The diagnostic that followed confirmed the hypothesis.
