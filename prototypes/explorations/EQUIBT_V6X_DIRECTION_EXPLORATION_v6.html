      The problem

      The mortgage business was losing 30% of inbound leads before an application was lodged, unconditional decisions took more than five days, and a competitor had already reached a 60-minute approval. The gap was widening.

      What we did

      Mapped the end-to-end process across direct and broker channels, credit, and origination.       Lead leakage fell from 30% to 10%, the unconditional decision rate lifted roughly 13 points within three months, and the roadmap was approved at Board level. The teams now run the redesigned process and the improvement backlog themselves.

      THE PROBLEM WE SOLVE

      Most improvement programmes produce results. Fewer produce results that last.

      The number one failure mode of operational improvement is not bad methodology.

      It is abandonment.

      Most programmes follow the same arc. A firm arrives, runs a diagnostic, designs solutions, runs a pilot, then leaves. Twelve months later the operation has drifted back, because the programme disrupted a stable operation without building what replaces it.

      We built EQUIBT around a different model.

      Every engagement is scoped from the outset to end with your team running the improved operation, without us in the room.

      WHAT WE BELIEVE

      Four things we will not negotiate on.

      Measurement before intervention.

      We do not accept anecdotes about where the problem is. We measure it. Every engagement starts with a quantified baseline. Opinion without data is not a starting point, it is a bias.

      Root cause before solution.

      We do not design solutions until we have isolated the cause. Addressing symptoms is maintenance, not improvement, and a good solution to the wrong problem wastes budget and goodwill.

      Context shapes method.

      We keep the method constant and change how we apply it. The questions we ask in financial services are not the questions we ask in government. Generic application is not rigour.

      AI-ready, not AI-naive.

      We treat AI as an accelerant, not a replacement for operational rigour, and only when the process is stable, the governance is in place, and the team understands what the model is doing.
