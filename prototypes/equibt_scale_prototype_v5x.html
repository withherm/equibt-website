WHY THIS MATTERS

Pilot success does not guarantee scale success.

Deployment, not handover. Standardisation, automation, capability transfer, and improvement systems make the validated way of working independent.

The gap between a successful pilot and a successful organisation-wide deployment is where most improvement programmes die. The pilot works because the pilot team was selected, motivated, and closely supported. Scaling to the full operation means deploying to teams that were not part of the design, may not understand the rationale, and have existing workflows they are comfortable with.

Standardisation is not about forcing uniformity. It is about identifying what must be consistent for the process to work (the non-negotiables) and what can flex to accommodate local context (the adaptables). Get this wrong and you either have rigid processes that break under local conditions, or inconsistent practices that make quality impossible to manage.

Automation deployment carries its own risks. Automating a broken process makes it break faster. Automating a validated process without governance means drift goes undetected at machine speed. SCALE deploys automation where it has been approved and where governance (from GOVERN) is already in place to catch problems.

Training and capability transfer is the final piece. Not classroom training that people forget. Embedded capability development that gives teams the skills to run the operation independently, manage variation, and continue improving after the engagement ends.

Standardisation without rigidity.

Retail and Consumer
SCALE | Operate
12 months sustained

Scaled the inventory model across regional operations.

Problem

A retail group had a successful pilot of a new inventory management process in one region. The pilot team had strong sponsor support, but each region had different legacy systems, local workarounds, and supervisor routines. The risk was that a rigid rollout would fail locally, while loose adoption would make performance impossible to manage.

Approach

We ran standardisation workshops with each regional team to define the non-negotiables and adaptables. The standardisation playbook set the core process, control points, and escalation paths, while allowing local variations that did not compromise quality. Supervisor coaching replaced classroom handover so the operating rhythm was learned in the flow of work.

Outcomes

All regions moved to the same operating model with local adaptables made explicit. The internal supervisors owned the playbook, coached the routines, and kept the process inside control thresholds after the rollout team stepped away.

Financial Services
SCALE | Operate
68% routine volume

Embedded governed automation across decision teams.

Problem

A financial services operation had a validated AI-assisted decision process and an approved technology build. The challenge was deployment across multiple teams with different decision habits, different exception patterns, and a high need for oversight. The automation could not simply be switched on and left to run.

Approach

We deployed the automation with human oversight protocols, drift detection checkpoints, and team-lead escalation paths. Team leads were trained as first-line responders for model edge cases, not passive users of a tool. A monthly performance review cadence connected the automation data back into the continuous improvement system.

Outcomes

Routine decisions moved into governed automation while human capacity shifted to complex cases requiring judgement. The teams kept the review cadence after deployment and could identify, escalate, and resolve model edge cases without waiting for external support.
