The improvement conversation typically follows a predictable pattern. A symptom appears - reports are late, decisions take time, customers complain about something. A hypothesis forms: it must be a process problem, or a structure problem, or a resourcing problem. A budget is approved. A solution is funded. And then, six months in, the improvement project stalls. An operations diagnostic breaks this pattern. It is structured, evidence-based mapping of how your operation actually works - not how the flowchart says it should work. The diagnostic identifies the highest-value improvement opportunities AND the organisation's readiness to act on them independently. It ends not with generic recommendations, but with a prioritised roadmap - a view of what to act on first, sequenced by impact and the effort required from your team to sustain it.

OUR WORK

Proven in practice.

Commercial Operations
PLAN | Measure
60% reduction

Standardised commercial planning nationally.

Problem

A national financial services group had no consistent plan/do/review methodology across its state-based sales functions. Each state team ran their own reporting approach and review cadence. Senior leadership was fielding ad-hoc reporting requests weekly because no standard output existed. The business had grown rapidly and the planning infrastructure had not kept pace.

Approach

Mapped the end-to-end commercial planning process across all state teams. Identified where reporting diverged and where decisions were being made without consistent data. Worked with the commercial leadership team to design standardised forums, decision rights, and a single reporting pack. Piloted the new cadence with two state teams before national rollout. The team sustained the approach for two years without external support.

Outcomes

Ad-hoc reporting requests eliminated as a recurring drain on leadership time.

Government and Public Sector
PLAN | Measure
183 to 30 categories

Identified and specified high-volume inbound calls for automation.

Problem

1,770 enquiries per month. 183-category taxonomy driving inconsistency. More than a third of inbound calls misdirected or out-of-jurisdiction, all handled manually. No agreed KPIs. Informal quality assurance.

Approach

Analysed enquiry volume across the full 183-category taxonomy to identify true call types versus classification noise. Ran structured root cause analysis on the out-of-jurisdiction volume to understand why it was arriving and being handled manually. Delivered a 12-recommendation roadmap including an IVR automation specification, simplified 30-category taxonomy, a quality assurance framework, and a revised workforce model.

Outcomes

The enquiry team left with a simplified classification model, IVR automation specification, quality assurance framework, and sequenced roadmap.
