The diagnostic found 47 problems. The real number was 6.

4 min read

Forty-seven issues. Six operational areas. The natural response was a 47-item project plan.

We spent a week mapping cause and effect chains instead.

TL;DR

  • Root causes drive multiple symptoms. Fixing one root cause dissolves 5-8 related problems without separate work.
  • Diagnostics reveal cause and effect chains. Most problems cluster around 3-6 structural issues.
  • Treating symptoms individually is slow. Identifying causes first changes the scale of the remediation effort by 70 percent.
  • Quick wins build momentum. Start with the root causes that dissolve the most symptoms.
  • Project scope based on causes is smaller, faster, and more targeted than scope based on the symptom list.

A diagnostic is a map of causes and symptoms, not a priority list. When you identify which problems are root causes and which are symptoms of those causes, a 47-item project plan usually becomes 6 items. The prioritisation question is not "what is most important" but "what generates the most other problems when left unfixed".

An energy retailer received a diagnostic report with 47 identified issues across 6 operational areas. The instinct was straightforward: build a 47-item project plan and resource it proportionally. The leadership team asked a different question. Which of the 47 problems were symptoms of a smaller set of root causes?

We spent a week mapping cause and effect chains. Each problem statement became a question: is this a cause or a symptom? For every symptom, we traced it back to its source. The pattern became clear. Six root cause issues were driving 38 of the 47 problems. Fixing those six would dissolve 38 issues without additional work. The remaining 9 were independent problems requiring direct fixes.

The project plan became 6 items, not 47. Delivered in 14 weeks instead of the estimated 18 months.

The pattern behind this:

Most diagnostic findings are symptoms. The causes are the ones worth finding first.

Diagnostic findings look like a checklist of independent problems. They are actually a map of causes and symptoms stacked on top of each other. A single data quality issue causes problems in reporting, in decision making, in reconciliation, and in customer facing systems. Each of those appears as a separate problem in the diagnostic. Fix the data quality issue and all four problems improve without additional work.

Most leaders see the list and count the items. Experienced diagnosticians see the list and count the causes. Those two numbers are almost never the same. A government agency's diagnostic found 34 issues in their grants administration process. Mapping the cause and effect chains revealed that 28 of those issues were caused by a single upstream problem: applications could not be classified into the correct grant category until after they were submitted, which created rerouting, reassessment, and queue backlogs throughout the process. Fixing the classification step at intake resolved 28 issues. The remaining 6 required direct attention.

Treating symptoms individually is expensive and slow. You fix one problem and another one emerges because you have not addressed the underlying cause. You improve a process and six months later the backlog is back because the cause of the backlog is still there. Identifying causes first is harder work upfront. It requires looking past the problem statement to the system condition that created it. But it changes the scale of the remediation effort. A 47-item project plan compresses to 6 items. A 12 month timeline becomes 3 months. A team burns out trying to fix 47 things. A team builds momentum fixing 6 things and seeing 38 problems disappear as a side effect.

One thing to try:

For each finding, ask: is this a cause or a symptom?

Take your diagnostic findings and for each one, ask: is this a cause or a symptom? For every symptom, trace it back to the root cause that generates it. You will find that most symptoms cluster around a small number of causes. Fix the causes first and track how many symptoms dissolve without additional work.

A 47-item remediation plan usually contains about 6 real problems and 41 consequences of them.
