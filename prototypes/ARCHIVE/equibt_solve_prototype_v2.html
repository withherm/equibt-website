Why start here

Treating symptoms is why improvements do not hold.

Most operational interventions address the visible problem, the symptom, without establishing what is driving it. The result is a fix that works temporarily and then regresses. The symptom returns, sometimes worse, because the cause is still active.

SOLVE is the phase that breaks that cycle. Using structured analytical methods, we identify the actual causes of your performance gaps. We then design a future state that closes those causes, not just their effects. And we cost it, sequence it, and build the business case that gets it funded.

The output of SOLVE is not a set of recommendations. It is a designed future state with a costed improvement plan and a sequenced roadmap for implementation.

Why EQUIBT?

Causes, not symptoms.

We do not move to solutions until we have confirmed the causes. Structured root cause analysis using Ishikawa, 5-Why, and data stratification ensures the solution addresses what is actually driving the problem.

Designed future state, not a list of actions.

SOLVE produces a designed future state: the specific process changes, capability investments, and system modifications required to close the performance gap. Not a slide deck of suggestions. A specification your team can build.

Capability transfer, not dependency.

SOLVE is designed so your internal team can understand, own, and maintain the analytical methods we use. We work alongside your people, not instead of them, so the capability stays in the organisation after we leave.

What we do

Three activities. Causes to solutions.

01

Root Cause Analysis

Using structured analytical methods, we identify the causal chain behind your performance gaps. We do not stop at the first plausible cause. We use data stratification, fishbone analysis, and 5-Why to get to the actual driver. This is the work that separates a proper improvement from a temporary fix.

Root Cause Analysis illustration

02

Future State Design

Based on confirmed root causes, we design the future state: the specific changes to process, capability, and system that will close the performance gap. The design is detailed enough to build from, not aspirational enough to argue about. It includes a prioritised improvement sequence.

Future State Design illustration

03

Cost-Benefit Analysis

Every improvement has a cost and a return. We quantify both. The cost-benefit analysis maps the investment required against the value recovered, giving your leadership team the information they need to make a funding decision with confidence. Includes sensitivity analysis on key assumptions.

The work

Recent engagements.

Financial Services SOLVE

47%

Processing errors reduced by 47% after root cause isolation identified a missing decision gate in the exception handling process.

High error rate in a claims processing function. Training had been deployed twice with no improvement. The problem was attributed to skill gaps.

Root cause analysis identified that the decision gate for exception routing was missing - claims were being processed by whoever picked them up, not by the team qualified to handle exceptions. Process redesign added the decision gate. Error rate dropped within four weeks.
Government and Public Sector SOLVE

3

Three redesigned processes delivered for a regulatory agency. All three sustained over 18 months post-engagement.

A regulatory agency needed to redesign its three highest-volume processes after a diagnostic identified systemic rework. Previous improvement attempts had failed because root cause analysis was skipped.

Structured root cause analysis using five-why methodology identified the common cause across all three processes: unclear decision rights at the point of exception. Future state design addressed decision rights first, then process flows. Capability was transferred to the internal team.
