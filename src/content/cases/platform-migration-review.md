problemFull: "The insurer was mid-way through renewing its core systems across eight products on a new platform. Two products had launched and the vendor had estimated a further fifteen months and a substantial spend to deliver the rest. The warning signs were already there: the system rated poor to uncertain across five capability dimensions, project management was ad hoc across every measure, delivery depended on two or three individuals, and the wider business had not bought into the migration. An independent review was needed before the next phase committed any more money."
approachFull: "We ran a six-week review in two parts. Discovery meant deep-dive interviews with internal and external stakeholders, a documentation inventory, and a structured assessment of five system areas and five project management dimensions. We identified the concerns that mattered most: cost and schedule underestimation, thin business-analyst resourcing, unstructured user acceptance testing, and named key-person risks that were neither documented nor cross-trained. We built 27 recommendations across six clusters, then validated the full set with stakeholders in a prioritisation workshop and mapped them by ease and impact. The output was a strategic action programme sequenced into must-have, should-have, and nice-to-have."
resultFull: "The leadership team received 27 prioritised recommendations across six clusters, plus a full read on platform capability, project management maturity, and key-person risk before the next phase began. The critical key-person risks were named and a remediation path defined before any of them could bite. The team held a clear, sequenced action programme it could execute itself, and the evidence to commit to the next phase with eyes open."
