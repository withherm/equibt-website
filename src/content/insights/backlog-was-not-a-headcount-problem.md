## What we did, and what we found.

A construction company ran a busy contract administration function: variations, approvals, and the paperwork that moves a project from agreed to built. Over several months the backlog had climbed and the team was visibly underwater. Leadership read it the way most leaders would. Volume is up, the team cannot keep pace, so we need more people and a better way to schedule them. A budget for twelve coordinators and a rostering tool was approved in principle. Before any of it was committed, we ran a short framing exercise.

We took a sample of the backlog and asked one question of every item: is this value-adding work the project actually needs, or is it non-value-added work? Rework, chasing missing information, re-keying the same data, handling the same approval twice. The split was stark. Most of the backlog was not new work waiting to be done. It was the same work going around more than once. Variations came back because they were submitted without the information needed to approve them. The same approval was logged in three systems by hand. Coordinators spent their day chasing details that should have arrived with the request.

The project we ended up running was not a hiring round. It was the removal of the non-value-added work itself: fixing the front end so variations arrived complete, eliminating the duplicate approval, and cutting the re-keying between systems. The backlog came down without the twelve hires.

## The pattern to avoid.

**Resourcing a backlog before defining what it is actually made of.**

A backlog is just a queue, and a queue grows for two completely different reasons: too little capacity, or too much work arriving per item. From the outside they look identical. Both show up as a pile that never goes down and a team that never catches up. The instinct, more hands, only fixes one of them. If the queue is full of waste, adding capacity raises the cost of the operation and hides the real problem behind a bigger payroll.

The team naming the problem cannot usually see this, because they are inside the work. The symptom they feel is not enough of us. The cause is harder to see from where they stand. Only framing the problem, opening the backlog and sorting value from waste, tells you which queue you are actually looking at.
