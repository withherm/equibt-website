## What we did, and what we found.

A major health insurer had selected a workflow automation tool. The vendor was locked in. The purchase order was approved. Implementation was scheduled to begin in four weeks. We ran a three-week diagnostic before go-live.

The diagnostic mapped the full claims assessment workflow end to end: volume patterns, queue depths, where applications sat, where quality checks happened, and what happened when applications were returned due to quality issues. By day twenty, the pattern was clear. The bottleneck was not the workflow. It was the assessment team. Assessment officers were applying the rules inconsistently. When guidelines could be read two ways, the rework was unpredictable. Some applications passed on the first check. Others went through four rounds of rework before approval. The workflow tool would not change this. It would automate a broken process, producing wrong outputs faster.

The implementation was redesigned. The automation was still deployed, but the underlying consistency problem was addressed first.

## The pattern to avoid.

**Committing to a solution before confirming the problem is what the solution actually fixes.**

Solution selection before diagnosis feels like progress because procurement is visible and tangible. Budgets are committed. Implementation dates are set. A diagnostic is invisible. It produces a report that may recommend pause and reconsider. This does not feel like progress.

But a diagnostic is risk management, not delay. The cost of a three-week diagnostic is less than the cost of discovering three months into implementation that you are automating the wrong thing. When the tool goes live on a broken process, you have a faster way to produce wrong outputs and correct them. The team blames the tool. The underlying problem remains. And you now have a committed contract and a sunk implementation to manage on top of the original problem.
