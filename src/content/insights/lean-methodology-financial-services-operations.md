## What we did, and what we found.

A bank's trade settlement team was running a 12 percent error rate. Settlements were processed and returned for correction. The standard response was more checking: add another review step, second pairs of eyes. The error rate stayed around 11 to 12 percent. More training and stricter controls had not changed it. This did not make sense unless training was not the constraint.

A value stream map of the settlement workflow was run instead. Every step, every data entry point, every copy, verification, and reformat. By day five the pattern emerged. Six non-value-adding handoffs in the workflow. At each handoff, data was being re-entered, reformatted, or verified by a second person because the upstream source was unreliable. Traders submitted data in different formats. The settlement team standardised it. The reconciliation team reformatted it again. The reporting team verified it a third time.

Four of the six handoffs were eliminated by fixing the upstream data source. Error rate dropped to 2.8 percent. Settlement time reduced by 1.5 days. Headcount did not change.

## The pattern to avoid.

**Adding verification and training to a flow problem rather than mapping where information loses integrity.**

Lean was developed to manage material flow in manufacturing. The principles are simple: identify what adds value, remove what does not, improve the flow. The same principles apply to information. Information has the same properties as material. It can be delayed, reworked, handled unnecessarily, batched when it should flow continuously, or sit in a queue waiting for the next step.

Back office work is information flow, not material flow. Waste categories are the same: delay, rework, unnecessary handoffs, and verification loops. These wastes are harder to see in back office than in manufacturing. You can walk a production line and see inventory piling up. In a back office, work piles up in systems and inboxes. But it is still piling up. Adding a sixth verification step to a workflow that has five already is not risk management. It is evidence that nobody has mapped where the information loses integrity.
