## What we did, and what we found.

A federal government agency ran an 18-month CI engagement across their grants and compliance teams. At month 12, the team leads were running their own improvement cycles with the external team in an advisory role only. They identified the problems, designed the analysis, led the team through root cause work, and owned the measurement.

At month 18, the engagement closed. At a follow-up review at month 24, the teams had completed two further improvement cycles independently. One reduced processing time by 18 percent. One identified and resolved a data quality issue that had been creating rework for years. Nobody had asked them to. They identified the problem, ran the cycle, and resolved it.

The sequence that produced this outcome: months 1 to 4, the external team led improvement cycles and team members participated. Months 5 to 8, team members co-led cycles with external support. Months 9 to 12, team members led cycles with the external team observing. Months 13 to 18, team members led cycles independently. That four-month handover sequence was built into the programme design at the start.

## The pattern to avoid.

**Designing the engagement to deliver improvement rather than to transfer improvement capability.**

When a consultant leads improvement cycles and the team participates, the team learns what to do. They do not learn that it is their job to do it. Knowledge transfers. Responsibility does not. At month 18 the consultant leaves. The problem-solving responsibility reverts to whoever owned it before. That person is now responsible for running improvement cycles on top of their existing job. When that is the design, the cycles stop. Six months later, the organisation has reverted.

This is not a failure of the consultant or the team. It is a design failure. Capability transfers when the team leads improvement cycles, not when they participate in them. If the engagement timeline does not include the four-month handover sequence before it closes, capability does not transfer.
