## What we did, and what we found.

A major bank piloted a redesigned mortgage processing workflow in a single high-performing branch. The pilot ran for 12 weeks with a team of 9 processors selected by the branch manager. Turnaround dropped from 14 days to 5.6 days. Error rates fell from 8.3 percent to 2.1 percent. The pilot was declared a success and the executive approved a national rollout across 34 branches.

Eight branches into the rollout, turnaround was averaging 11.2 days. Error rates were at 6.4 percent. The programme team investigated and found four conditions present in the pilot that did not exist in the wider operation. The pilot branch had selected their strongest processors. The branch manager had personally attended every daily standup. Exception cases had been quietly routed to a senior team member outside the pilot workflow. And the pilot team had been given a temporary exemption from two compliance checkpoints that were still mandatory for every other branch.

None of these conditions were documented in the pilot report. The report showed the result: 5.6 days, 2.1 percent errors. It did not show the enabling conditions that made the result possible.

## The pattern to avoid.

**Reporting the pilot result without documenting the conditions that produced it.**

Pilots are designed to test whether a new way of working delivers better outcomes. That is a useful question. But it is not the only question. The second question, which most pilots do not answer, is whether the conditions that enabled those outcomes can be reproduced at operational scale. The bank's pilot answered the first question convincingly. But the conditions that produced that result were exceptional. The team was hand-picked. Management attention was concentrated on nine people instead of distributed across 200. Compliance checkpoints were relaxed.

When the rollout team tried to replicate the workflow in branches with average performers, standard compliance requirements, distributed management attention, and no informal exception routing, the workflow could not deliver the same outcome. The pilot had proved a concept. It had not proved scalability.
