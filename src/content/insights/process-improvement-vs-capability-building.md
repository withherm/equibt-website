## What we did, and what we found.

At a utilities company in New South Wales, the billing dispute process was redesigned. Results were strong at the three-month mark. Cycle time down, rework reduced, customer complaints fell. At nine months, the metrics had drifted back. An internal review found the team had been following the new process because the consultant was there, not because they understood why it worked. When variation crept in, nobody corrected it because nobody had learned to diagnose it.

The team knew how to follow the new process. They did not know how to maintain it.

## The pattern to avoid.

**Designing a new process without building the capability to keep it running after the engagement closes.**

In a pure process improvement model, the consultant diagnoses the problem, designs the solution, and helps the team implement it. The team observes. The metric improves. The consultant leaves. Eighteen months later, when the next problem emerges, the team escalates it hoping someone will hire a consultant again. In a capability building model, the team learns the diagnostic method. They practice it on the original problem. They run the first improvement cycle independently. When the next problem emerges, they have the capability to at least try to solve it.

Capability is not a single thing. It is the combination of method, tools, management system, and experience. Method is the structured way of approaching a problem. Tools are the frameworks that support it. The management system is the weekly meeting where the team reviews data and the quarterly review of whether improvements are holding. You cannot teach experience, but you can create the conditions for the team to build it.
