## What we did, and what we found.

A large health insurer had board approval for a major systems transformation. The business case was solid. Budget was approved. The programme kicked off. Six weeks in, the programme discovered what a two-week diagnostic would have found before it started. Core customer data was too inconsistent to migrate cleanly. Names, addresses, policy numbers: basic data was recorded in conflicting formats across systems. The transformation paused for 14 weeks while data remediation happened.

The delay cost A$1.2 million. The readiness diagnostic that would have found the problem would have cost A$35,000 and taken two weeks.

## The pattern to avoid.

**Treating board approval as a readiness signal when it only confirms appetite.**

Appetite and budget are not readiness. Stability, data quality, and capacity are. Readiness has three critical dimensions. Operational capacity means the team has availability to participate, not willingness, availability. If every team member is committed to business as usual, they cannot participate in transformation. Data readiness means a baseline exists. You cannot improve what you cannot measure. Process stability means the current operation is stable enough to absorb change. If business as usual is chaotic, adding transformation will break it.

Most transformation programmes that fail do so before they start. The methodology was sound. The team was competent. The organisation just was not ready.
