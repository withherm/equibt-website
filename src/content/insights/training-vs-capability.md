## What we did, and what we found.

A major Australian insurer sent 14 people through an accredited Lean Green Belt programme over 6 months. The training was well delivered. The participants were engaged. At the 12-month mark, there was no measurable change in operational performance in the teams where the trained staff worked. An internal review found that none of the 14 had applied the methodology to a real problem since completing the training. There were no application projects. There was no expectation that the training would produce measurable outcomes.

The organisation had purchased knowledge with no mechanism to convert it into practice.

A logistics company ran the same type of training but attached a project before training began. The team identified a real problem: order picking was taking longer than expected for certain order types. During the training, every concept was applied to understanding their picking problem. They measured current state, mapped the process, identified root causes, and designed improvements. Picking time dropped 18 percent. When training finished, the team had not just learned the method. They had proven they could use it.

## The pattern to avoid.

**Running training without an application project attached before it starts.**

Training is what happens in a room. An instructor teaches the method. Participants work through exercises. They return to work with emails waiting, crises, and a backlog of requests. The method they learned is abstract. The work they need to do is concrete. Without structured support to apply the method to their real problems, the method becomes an academic exercise. Within weeks, the team has reverted to solving problems the way they always did because that is faster and more familiar.

The gap between training and application is not intention. Most participants want to use what they learned. Applying the method to a real problem is harder than doing it in a classroom. Diagnosis requires looking at actual data, not a case study. Root cause analysis means having difficult conversations with people who will be affected. Measurement means building systems you will live with for months. Without a specific project attached to the training, participants default to the familiar. Knowledge fades in 90 days because there is no reason to hold onto it.
