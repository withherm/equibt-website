## What we did, and what we found.

A utilities company's senior operations coordinator resigned after 11 years. A thorough SOP folder existed. The replacement was capable and experienced. Within 3 weeks, three recurring situations had stalled.

A specific billing dispute type required a system workaround known only to the departing coordinator. A customer escalation pathway for a defined segment bypassed the standard process, and was undocumented. A monthly reconciliation task did not appear in any SOP because it had emerged gradually over years. All three were load-bearing. None were written down.

The SOP folder captured the standard path. The coordinator carried the non-standard paths: the exceptions, the workarounds, the judgment calls that had emerged gradually and were never documented because they worked. Those paths are often more critical than the standard path, because they are what experienced people handle when the operation gets difficult.

## The pattern to avoid.

**Assuming the SOP folder contains what the experienced person actually does.**

Processes evolve incrementally. A new requirement appears and a workaround is created. A system limitation emerges and someone develops a manual step that bypasses it. A customer relationship requires special handling and a pathway is informally created. Over time these become load-bearing. The operation depends on them. But they are never documented because they emerged gradually and nobody noticed they had become standard.

When the experienced person is managing them, the non-standard paths are invisible. The SOP folder is complete and up to date. The operation runs smoothly. Then the experienced person leaves and suddenly the non-standard paths are load-bearing with nobody knowing how to run them.

A capability transfer programme solves this while the person is still there. It documents the non-standard paths. It creates a day-one runbook that tells the new person what to do, not a training manual, but a practical guide for the first day and the first week. This is not a two-week handover: it is an eight-week programme.
