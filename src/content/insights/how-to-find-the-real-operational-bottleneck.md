## What we did, and what we found.

A mid-tier Australian lender had been running 23 days to approve a mortgage against a 14-day target. The credit team was the bottleneck. Everyone agreed. The answer seemed obvious: look at credit, look at headcount, look at the queue.

The full process was mapped instead. Not the procedure document. What was actually happening at each step.

Credit assessment could not begin until a complete document pack arrived from the customer. The document request process had no standard checklist, no tracking, no follow-up. Each broker requested documents differently. Customers submitted incomplete packs because nobody had told them clearly what was needed. Some customers responded to three or four separate requests for the same application.

By the time a complete pack reached credit, the application was already 11 days old. Credit assessed it in four days, inside their target. The total cycle was 23 days because the upstream step was unmanaged and invisible.

The credit team was not the bottleneck. They were receiving broken input and working efficiently with it.

## The pattern to avoid.

**Measuring from where the problem is visible, not from where it starts.**

This pattern appears in almost every operation: measurement starts at the step that is visible, the queue, the dashboard flag, the team named in the weekly report. It rarely tracks what happens before that step. And because cross-team processes rarely have a single owner, nobody is accountable for the quality of what moves between them.

Fixing the document request step reduced the total cycle from 23 days to 15. Standard checklist, clear broker guide, automated follow-up at day three. Credit did not change. Credit headcount did not change. The credit team had carried the label of bottleneck for two years while nobody examined what was arriving in their queue.
