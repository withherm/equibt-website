## What we did, and what we found.

A mid-tier bank had scoped an operational reengineering of their customer onboarding process: new roles, new technology, new process design across 4 teams, 20-week timeline. A three-week diagnostic ran before the reengineering design began. It revealed that 60 percent of the onboarding friction was caused by a single data validation issue upstream. This was fixable in two weeks with a system configuration change. The reengineering scope reduced by half. The two-week fix was implemented first. The remaining reengineering work was more targeted and took 8 weeks instead of the planned 20.

A telecommunications company looked at their network provisioning taking 6 weeks and decided it needed reengineering. They removed 6 approval steps. Provisioning time dropped from 6 weeks to 5. A diagnostic would have found the constraint: network equipment was backordered for an average of 4 weeks. Approval was a symptom, not the constraint. The reengineering fixed the wrong problem.

## The pattern to avoid.

**Starting reengineering from a problem statement rather than a diagnostic.**

Time pressure is usually the reason experienced leaders skip the diagnostic. An executive sees chaos and wants to move fast. A diagnostic feels like delay. But the diagnostic is not overhead. It replaces assumptions with evidence and almost always changes the scope, the sequencing, or both.

The stated problem is often a symptom. A call centre wanted to reengineer for more calls per agent. The diagnostic revealed that complex calls were mixed with simple calls in an unprioritised queue. Separating call types and rerouting reduced handling time for simple calls without reengineering anything. A retail company's leadership thought inventory was a people problem. Staff were not following procedure. The diagnostic revealed staff were deliberately not following procedure because it was slower and more error-prone than their workaround. The procedure had been designed for a different product mix five years earlier.

Reengineering designed without a diagnostic is designed around the loudest assumption.
