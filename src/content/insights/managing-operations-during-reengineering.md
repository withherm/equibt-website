## What we did, and what we found.

A health insurer was processing 2,200 claims per week. The claims handling process needed to be reengineered. Stopping was not an option. The approach: new claims arriving after a defined date ran on the new process in a controlled cohort. Existing claims ran on the old process through to resolution. Both cohorts were measured simultaneously across handling time, accuracy, and customer contact rate.

By week 6, the new cohort was consistently outperforming the old. By week 8, the evidence was clear. Full cutover happened at week 8.

The parallel run had another effect. Staff who watched the new process running in real conditions alongside the old process saw the difference before they were asked to commit to it. By the time cutover happened, the team was already mentally running the new process. The cutover itself was operational, not organisational.

## The pattern to avoid.

**Reengineering as a switchover: a single date when everything moves to the new design.**

The parallel run removes the risk leaders fear most: a failed redesign that stops the business. When the cutover is a single date, the organisation is committed to the new process before it has been proven at volume. If the redesign underperforms, the operation is already on it. If there are problems not visible in controlled testing, they emerge at full scale.

Reengineering is not a switchover. It is a controlled handover from one proven process to another. The cutover criteria become the objective trigger for the decision. When the criteria are met, cutover is automatic. When they are not, the programme has an objective reason to extend the parallel run. That removes emotion and guesswork from the decision moment.
